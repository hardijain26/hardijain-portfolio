Building Pantry Vault, a pantry app for vegetarian kitchens

A personal project. I wanted to find out how far a product manager can take an idea with AI tools and no engineer: from one prompt in Google AI Studio to an app with sign-in, a database and a live URL.

01 · WHY THIS ONE

It started with a packet of spinach.

In my kitchen, paneer and curd go off at the back of the fridge, the spinach wilts before anyone cooks it, and the dal runs out on a Wednesday night. We still end up ordering in.

I didn’t start with market research. I picked a problem I see every week and can test on myself before asking anyone else to.

02 · THE FIRST VERSION

Google AI Studio gave me eight tabs in an afternoon.

I described the app in plain English and got back a React app with a pantry, recipes, voice input, a community feed, a seasonal produce map and WhatsApp alerts. The demo looked ready to launch.

Then I read the code. Everything was saved in one browser, so clearing it wiped the pantry. The community feed only showed my own posts to me. The “automated” WhatsApp alert was a pop-up that said it had sent something. The recipe-link analyser never opened the link.

03 · THE CUT

Eight tabs became four.

I kept what serves one loop: know what you have, cook what’s about to go off, restock what’s run out. Pantry, stock alerts, recipes from the pantry, and voice.

I deleted the community feed, the seasonal map and the link analyser. I also removed the medical-conditions profile. An app that estimates nutrition with AI has no business telling someone with diabetes what to eat.

04 · MAKING IT REAL

The part AI builders don’t give you.

Before anyone else could use it, the app needed accounts, a real database and a limit on AI spend. Sign-in is an emailed link. Each household can only read its own rows in the database. Every account gets 30 AI requests a day, so nobody can run up a bill.

I didn’t write this code myself. I directed an AI coding agent: decided what each piece had to do, reviewed what came back, and tested it on the live site.

05 · THE FEATURE I ADDED

Nobody types their groceries in.

Most pantry apps die at data entry, so I added a way around it: take a photo of a shop bill, or upload a screenshot of a Zepto or Blinkit order.

The AI keeps only the food, skips delivery fees and dish soap, reads “2 × 500 ml” as a litre of milk and guesses a use-by date for each item. Nothing is saved until you’ve seen the list and unticked anything it got wrong.

06 · WHEN THE FREE TIER SAID NO

Error 503: this model is experiencing high demand.

The first real scan failed. Google’s free Gemini tier was overloaded, and I have no budget for a paid API.

So the app now tries Gemini, then Groq, then OpenRouter, all on free tiers, and uses the first one that answers. Finding the error took longer than fixing it: after a few failures the browser had quietly started blocking the app’s pop-ups, so a failed import looked like a button that did nothing. Errors now show inside the app.

Screens from the live app, filled with sample data: the pantry with what’s expiring, a recipe built from what’s in it, and the restock list that goes out on WhatsApp.

A short walkthrough video is on its way.

08 · WHERE IT STANDS

Live, and so far I’m the only one using it.

The next step is 10 to 20 households I can talk to after their first week. Before I invite them I want two things in place: an “I cooked this” button that takes ingredients off the shelf, and a daily reminder about what’s about to expire.

The number I’ll watch is simple: how many people are still keeping their pantry up to date after week one. A pantry app with a stale pantry is useless, and paying for one would be worse.
